## Definition

Human-AI collaborative teaming refers to team configurations where both humans and AI agents participate as first-class peers — not as operators/tools or human-and-copilot dyads. All participants share the same communication medium, adopt distinct roles (e.g., CEO, developer, client), and coordinate through emergent social behavior rather than hard-coded orchestration. The AI agents must be capable of autonomous role-appropriate action, self-regulation (waiting for inputs before proceeding), and adaptation to human-directed changes in team dynamics.

## Key distinctions

- Differs from **copilot paradigms** (GitHub Copilot) where the human is always in the decision seat and AI is a tool within the IDE.
- Differs from **AI-only multi-agent systems** (MetaGPT, ChatDev) where humans are clients external to the agent loop.
- Differs from **dyadic human-AI interaction** research which studies one human interacting with one AI on narrow tasks.
